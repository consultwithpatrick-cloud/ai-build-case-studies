Live, in daily production

AI Reply Assistant

A human-in-the-loop reply assistant inside the team's existing messaging tool. Drafts are grounded in the team's own conversation history, a routing and caching layer keeps running cost low, and a nightly learning loop keeps the voice current. A person sends every reply.

Reply Training Console

A safe training environment that captures a distinct voice for each department without touching production. It ingests conversations, anchors per-department voice, and drafts reply options with full-conversation context.

Call-Time Dispatch Assistant

A booking panel that fills itself during the call. Identifies the caller, matches a qualified technician against the roster, and offers an arrival window from live road distances, so the job is booked while the customer is on the line.

Dispatch Understudy

A narrow, opt-in capture that turns an expert dispatcher's judgment into reviewable rules. Scheduling actions only, customer data masked, stays on company systems, proposes rather than acts.

Production Reliability Layer

Twenty-eight unattended workflows where health is proven by fresh dated output, an independent watchdog runs twice daily, silence raises an alarm, and recovery backfills the gap rather than skipping it.

How to read this portfolio

Three things worth knowing before you start.

The interesting part is the boundaries

Not that AI systems were built, but where the limits were drawn: a model that never touches a total, a capture tool that cannot become surveillance, a form field removed rather than validated, a watchdog that does not trust a job to report on itself.

Status labels are accurate

Live, in tuning, in build, and scoped mean different things and are used consistently. Nothing in build is dressed up as shipped, because the labels are the easiest thing in the world to check in a walkthrough.

Authorship is claimed. Ownership is not.
