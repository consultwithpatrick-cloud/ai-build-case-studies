Self-Serve Campaign Automation With a Human Approval Gate

A non-technical sender fills in a short form and picks a brand-locked template. The system assembles the campaign, renders it, and routes it to a named approver. Nothing schedules until a person signs off, and there is no field in the form that can break the brand.

The problem

Both obvious fixes make it worse.

  • Campaigns bottleneck on whoever knows the email platform. Requests queue behind one person, and time-sensitive campaigns go out late or quietly do not go out at all.
  • Give more people direct access and brand consistency degrades immediately. Typography, layout, disclaimers, and sending identity drift campaign by campaign, and no single instance looks bad enough to trigger a fix.
  • So the real constraint is uncomfortable: the answer cannot be more access, and it cannot be one gatekeeper. It has to be a path where the fast way and the correct way are the same way.

The solution

Make the form the guardrail.

A short form is the entire interface. The sender chooses a brand-locked template and supplies only the content that legitimately varies: audience, subject, and body copy. Everything structural is fixed inside the template and is not exposed as a field.

The system assembles the campaign deterministically, renders a real preview, and routes it to a named approver. Approval is a required step in the path, not a notification that can be missed. Only an approved campaign is scheduled, and it is scheduled through a single code path with no bypass.

The design principleIf a field cannot be wrong, it cannot be got wrong. Brand governance implemented as training is a hope. Implemented as an absent form field, it is a guarantee.

How it works

Six steps, one of which is a person.

FormAsk only for what varies

The sender provides audience, subject, and body content. Nothing else is requested, because nothing else should be theirs to decide.

TemplateBrand-locked by construction

Layout, typography, footer, legal disclaimers, and sending identity live in the template. No form field overrides them, so no submission can produce an off-brand campaign.

AssembleDeterministic build

The campaign is assembled the same way every time from the template and the submitted fields. Same input, same campaign, which is what makes a preview meaningful.

PreviewWhat is approved is what sends

The approver reviews the rendered campaign itself, not a summary or a description of it. There is no gap between the artefact reviewed and the artefact sent.

GateA named human approves

Approval is a required transition, not an alert someone might action. Without it the campaign does not reach the scheduler at all.

ScheduleOne path out

An approved campaign is scheduled through a single code path. There is no secondary route, manual override, or admin shortcut that skips the gate.

Where this system actually is

This system is in build. The form, the template lock, deterministic assembly, and approval routing are implemented. It is described here at the design level only. Production results will be added to this page when it has run long enough to have them, and not before.

Why it is included anyway

The interesting content is the design decision, not the outcome. Choosing to remove fields rather than add training is a repeatable pattern, and it is the part that transfers to another business.

What is deliberately absent

No language model sits in this path. Campaign assembly is templating and validation. Adding a model here would introduce variance into the one place the business needs none.
